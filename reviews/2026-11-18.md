Verdict: WARN
Summary: The collection and ranking calculations look sound, but passing mentions and repeated coverage make some rises easy to overstate.

## Collection
All three sources have all 21 days. No daily item total falls below half or above double its source’s median: 1,069 HN stories, 34 papers, and 3,470 models. Paper zeros occur only on weekends.

Model likes are unusually high on November 2 and 12, and low on November 15. These do not affect rankings, which use uploader counts. Lower recent model topic matches are expected while tags fill in.

## Matching
These are judgments from the supplied excerpts, not measured error rates.

- HN: funding 8/8, GPUs 8/8, world models 6/6, and AI chips 8/8 look relevant.
- Papers: science 4/4 and quantization about 7/8 look substantively relevant. Claude and GPUs each have about 4/8 strong matches; the remaining samples mostly mention the topic as background, a comparison, or equipment used.
- Models: AI chips 8/8 and synthetic data 8/8 look relevant. MCP has 7/8 supported matches and one uncertain speech model. Hallucination has 6/8 supported matches; the two `finecat` examples show no hallucination evidence in the displayed tags, which may be truncated.

Paper Claude and GPU matches exceed the roughly one-quarter threshold for weak topical relevance. For example, “Ropedia Xperience 2” mentions Claude in background about robot control, while “Weight-Space Programming Doesn't Need Transformers” says architectures “run fast on GPUs.” These are genuine mentions, but weak evidence that the papers focus on those topics. That distinction supports WARN rather than a clearly broken-count FAIL.

## Rankings
Today's displayed counts, shares, lifts, and ordering agree with the daily CSVs. The claim that GPUs are rising across all three sources also checks out.

No leading topic is dominated by one day. The leading science-paper topic has 25 recent matches, with no day contributing more than three.

Uploader concentration needs context: six of eight AI chip model samples are Xylolabs uploads, and MCP includes repeated variants from Revanth-ml and siva-0104. However, the published model rankings already count each uploader once per topic per day. AI chips’ 193 model matches over seven days become 55 uploader-days; MCP’s 50 become 24. The samples alone do not establish that one uploader drives the ranked rise.

HN coverage clusters around Anthropic’s IPO and Nvidia leadership news. Five funding samples concern the IPO. These are repeated events, but titles alone cannot establish duplicate submissions.

Yesterday’s changes are plausible rolling-window effects. Claude papers fell from 51 to 47 because five older matches left the window and one entered. Science stayed at 25 and moved above Claude.

One evidence inconsistency needs fixing: the HN GPU sample heading reports 239 seven-day matches, but November 12 through 18 sums to 231 in `topic_daily.csv`. The 30-day ranking total of 547 agrees.

## Suggested fixes
- Generate sample headings and ranking figures from the same frozen data and date boundaries; resolve the 239 versus 231 discrepancy.
- In paper matching code, distinguish passing abstract mentions from substantive topic matches, or explicitly label these figures as keyword mentions. Recompute both comparison periods if matching rules change.
- Add uploader concentration indicators alongside model rankings, retaining the existing uploader-per-day counting.
- Deduplicate HN submissions by canonical article URL and distinguish repeated coverage of one event from separate developments.
